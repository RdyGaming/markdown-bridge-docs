Accessibility statement

Last updated: 16 November 2026

We want everyone to be able to use Markdown Bridge. The app and this site are built to meet the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2 at level AA.

What we built in

  • Everything works with a keyboard. Tabs follow the standard pattern: arrow keys move between tabs, Home and End jump to the first and last.
  • Choosing files does not require drag and drop. Buttons do the same job.
  • Every field has a visible label. Errors are written in text, tied to their field, and focus moves to the field.
  • Progress and results are announced to screen readers. When a run ends, focus moves to the result heading.
  • Text and controls meet contrast minimums: at least 4.5 to 1 for text and 3 to 1 for control borders and focus rings.
  • The layout reflows down to 320 pixels wide and works at 200 percent zoom. Targets are at least 24 by 24 pixels.
  • The app follows your Confluence theme, including dark mode, respects reduced motion, and supports forced colors mode.
  • Status is never shown by color alone. Each result has a text label.

How it was checked

The interface was reviewed against the WCAG 2.2 AA success criteria, color pairs were measured, and keyboard and focus behavior were tested in a browser. It has not yet been through an audit by an independent accessibility specialist or a formal round of testing with assistive technology users. We will update this statement when that happens.

Known limits

  • The app runs inside Confluence, so the surrounding navigation and menus are Atlassian's and follow Atlassian's own accessibility work.
  • Choosing a whole folder relies on a browser feature that some browsers and assistive technologies handle unevenly. Choosing a zip file is an equal alternative.
  • Markdown Bridge converts what you give it. It does not add missing alt text or fix heading order in your content.

Tell us about a barrier

If something does not work for you, email doustd+redheaddev@gmail.com with the subject "Accessibility". Tell us what you were trying to do and what assistive technology and browser you use. We aim to reply within 2 business days and treat accessibility barriers as defects, not feature requests.
